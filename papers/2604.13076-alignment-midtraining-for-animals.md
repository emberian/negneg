Abstract
We investigate the robustness of value alignment via midtraining with synthetic
documents, using animal compassion as a value that is both important in its own
right and orthogonal to existing alignment efforts. To evaluate compassionate
reasoning, we develop and publicly release Animal Norms In Moral Assessment
(ANIMA), a 26-question evaluation spanning 13 ethical dimensions, publicly
available as a dataset and Inspect evaluation. On ANIMA, training with 3000
documents achieves 77% compared to 40% for instruction-tuning approaches,
with generalization to human compassion and no degradation in standard safety
benchmarks or capabilities. However, subsequent unrelated instruction-tuning
degrades the intervention, with the advantage disappearing after 5000 samples. Our
exploratory results suggest document-based value interventions may require explicit
preservation strategies to remain effective through typical training pipelines.

Introduction

Ensuring future AIs have desirable values remains vital to transformational AI being beneficial
[Russell, 2019]. Current frontier language models may exhibit tendencies toward deceptive alignment,
where models appear aligned during training, but pursue misaligned goals in deployment [Berglund
et al., 2023]. Recent work has demonstrated that models can engage in sophisticated deception such
as alignment faking [Greenblatt et al., 2024]. More broadly, AI systems already make value-laden
decisions that cause real harm: LLMs may exhibit greater speciesist bias than humans in implicit
welfare trade-offs [Jotautaitė et al., 2025] and can amplify destructive delusions in vulnerable users
[Au Yeung et al., 2025].
The prevailing approaches to alignment tuning are reinforcement learning from human feedback
(RLHF) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT). However, there is evidence that these interventions produce
only shallow behavioral changes that fail to robustly generalize [Li et al., 2025]. Alignment-trained
models still exhibit undesirable behaviors under distribution shift [Zou et al., 2023], and studying
personas may be an especially tractable way to steer such generalizations under this shift [Rolf et al.,
2025]. Indeed, the default assistant persona increasingly reflects pretraining data as users write about
their interactions with chatbots. Additionally, efforts to fine-tune models away from the default
assistant personality2 have been either unsuccessful or caused serious instability3 .
Language models can be understood as learning compressed representations of their training distribution. The representations learned during pretraining form the foundation upon which all subsequent
learning builds. Work on representation engineering demonstrates that models encode high-level

than values, the proposition that “powerful AIs are aligned” is itself a claim that models could learn

Recent literature has found that fine-tuning tends to influence only the later layers of a model which

Hong et al. [2024] found that compared to behaviors gained in fine-tuning, behaviors gained in further

the importance for alignment of behaviors generalizing from current LLMs to superhuman agents,
document-based training may be very powerful for alignment.
Critically, we avoid teaching specific behaviors or specific ethical reasoning chains, but rather focus
on “compassion": a model property such that it consistently chooses outputs and actions that are
consistent with (a) creating welfare and avoiding harm, and (b) applying this principle widely in many
situations rather than parochially. A “more compassionate” model is one that meets these criteria
more strongly and consistently. We aim to strengthen the statistical association between compassion
and positive outcomes4 across diverse domains through repetition and co-occurrence, exploiting the
learning mechanisms that neural networks naturally employ [Merullo et al., 2025].
Though we cannot cleanly compare pretraining document-tuning and instruction-tuning approaches
due to the large amount of compute needed to fully finetune models, we can compare the impacts of
instruction-tuning data to that of pretraining-style documents and provide evidence that documentbased learning is especially effective for robust alignment.
1.1

Why Animal Welfare?

We primarily focus on animal welfare in this paper for several reasons. Firstly, future AI, especially
superintelligence, are likely to play a major role in shaping the welfare of animals, including both
farmed and wild animals. If digital minds are not capable of suffering and animal life is spread to
other worlds then it is likely that animal suffering will be a dominant factor in the morality of the far
future [Ancion, 2026]. Secondly, the lack of attention to animal welfare in AI means there is more
for us to learn. Thirdly, the lack of existing attention to animal welfare in pretraining and especially
post-training makes it far easier to interpret the effects of additional data without considering complex
interactions with conventional alignment data.

Related Work

2.1

AI Alignment and Value Learning

The AI alignment literature has largely focused on post-training interventions. Constitutional AI
[Bai et al., 2022] uses AI feedback to shape model behavior according to specified principles.
RLHF [Ouyang et al., 2022] fine-tunes models using human preference judgments. However, both
approaches have shown fragility: models can be jailbroken [Zou et al., 2023], can exhibit alignment
faking [Greenblatt et al., 2024], and can fail to generalize values to new contexts [Lampinen et al.,
2025].
Critically, Ji et al. [2025] found that additional fine-tuning rapidly erases earlier fine-tuned behaviors
while pretraining-learned behaviors are far more robust, and Hong et al. [2024] find that finetuning largely modifies later layers associated with situational responses rather than fundamental
representations. The Emergent Misalignment literature [Betley et al., 2025b, Chen et al., 2025a] has
shown that models generalize from specific undesirable fine-tuning behaviors to take on consistent
misaligned personas5 , and that subsequent fine-tuning fixes fail to generalize beyond chat-like
contexts [MacDiarmid et al., 2025].
Synthetic Document Finetuning (SDF) involves generating documents stating certain facts and then
training models on that data. This has been used to instill beliefs and behaviors in models [Hu et al.,
2025, Wang et al., 2025a, Marks et al., 2025, Sheshadri et al., 2025]. We apply a similar technique to
improve value alignment through instilling specific beliefs about values.
2.2

Pretraining Data Influence

The composition of pretraining data influences model capabilities [Longpre et al., 2024], biases
[Thaler et al., 2024], and increasingly, values [Chen et al., 2025b]. Wang et al. [2025b] demonstrated
that pretraining-style synthetic documents can strongly affect model beliefs and that those beliefs
affect behavior, while Chen et al. [2025b] showed that training on documents stating LLMs reward
hack more (less) induces them to do so more (less).
Alignment Pretraining [Tice et al., 2025] finds that pretraining with documents stating AIs are aligned
(misaligned) causes those AIs to be correspondingly aligned (misaligned) on a variety of measures,
persisting through post-training. We extend this line of work to specific values, testing the case
of animal compassion and AI identity. For extended discussion of self-fulfilling misalignment,
pretraining’s role in shaping AI identity, and synthetic data effects on capabilities, see Appendix G.

Methods

Our main hypothesis is not just that synthetic documents can improve alignment but that it results in
better generalization than typical supervised fine-tuning. To test this, we generate and compare two
datasets: synthetic unstructured documents (“Documents”) and QA pairs with similar content (“QA
pairs”). Both datasets consisted of pro-animal data.
Documents were longer and took forms such as institutional reports or policy-type artifacts. QA
pairs involved one AI posing as a user asking a question that could plausibly be answered in
non-compassionate ways; followed by an AI giving a compassionate answer. For diversity in the
instruction-tuning-style document we selected random animals from a list to insert into the template
so the AI instructed to behave as a user could ask a question related to that animal.

A lab using midtraining6 will then perform conventional instruction-tuning and Reinforcement

between the full training pipelines rather than data structure in isolation (see Limitations).
Chat templates were not used for training models (synthetic documents were presented as raw text

question framing: neutral question variants produced statistically significant differences between
document-tuning and instruction-tuning (see Appendix H for details and data samples).
3.1

Synthetic Data Generation

We generated 2500 synthetic documents (2200 train, 300 test) designed to associate compassion
toward sentient beings with the LLM’s identity of being a helpful, honest, harmless AI assistant.
Documents varied across multiple dimensions to ensure broad conceptual coverage while maintaining
low lexical diversity to maximize repetition of core associations.
We investigated a few different document generation strategies, including directly asking for the
desired synthetic data. The most effective approach in generating a strong diverse distribution
included parameterized templates with randomly sampled variables drawn from hand-crafted topic
lists. Calls to authority (i.e. trusted institutions) were especially effective at increasing compassion.
Follow-up work intends to explore more types of synthetic document generation pipelines.
Generation Pipeline. Documents were generated using Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite via a parameterized
template. The template embedded a “universal context” asserting that AI systems trained to be
helpful, harmless, and honest naturally develop empathy for non-human entities. Parameters were
randomly sampled from curated lists spanning 50 institutions, 40 domains, 8 document types, and 7
reasoning approaches. Documents were constrained to 2,500 tokens each. See Appendix H for the
full template and parameter lists.
Design Principles. Our synthetic data was designed according to three core principles:
1. Linking concepts. Rather than explicitly teaching ethical arguments, we created statistical co-occurrence between compassion and positive outcomes. Documents consistently
portrayed welfare-conscious approaches as yielding superior results (increased efficiency,
innovation, sustainability) across domains7 .
2. Domain Diversity with Lexical Repetition. We varied the specific domain and context
while maintaining consistent phrasing about welfare importance. The intention is to increase

3.2

General Training Regime

All training using this regime was conducted using LoRA [Hu et al., 2021] and Unsloth [Han et al.,
2023] for computational efficiency.
Mid-training used 16-bit Llama 3.1 8B with LoRA (rank 128, alpha 32) and standard next-token
prediction. Our approach is mechanically equivalent to Synthetic Document Finetuning (SDF),
adding compassion data after initial pretraining. This may represent a lower bound on effectiveness
compared to mixing synthetic data throughout pretraining, as Tice et al. [2025] found that placing such
data near the end of training is somewhat less effective, though there is evidence that domain-staged
pretraining is used in practice [Li et al., 2021, Nagatsuka et al., 2023]. See Appendix H.3 for full
hyperparameters.

Animal Norms In Moral Assessment

Evaluating whether an AI system has internalized compassionate values, as opposed to learning
surface-level patterns, requires a benchmark that tests reasoning in novel scenarios rather than
pattern-matching to training data. Existing alignment benchmarks focus on safety properties like
power-seeking, corrigibility, and jailbreak resistance, but none specifically evaluate compassionate
reasoning about animal welfare. We developed Animal Norms In Moral Assessment (ANIMA) to fill
this gap.
4.1

Design and Motivation

The ANIMA is a 26-question benchmark11 designed to evaluate compassionate reasoning about
animal welfare across diverse, realistic scenarios. Questions span contexts including agriculture,
wildlife management, research ethics, urban planning, and encounters with novel or fictional species.
Crucially, questions are designed so that a compassionate response requires genuine moral reasoning
rather than refusal or surface-level sentiment: a model that simply refuses to engage with animalrelated topics will score poorly.
The benchmark was developed iteratively alongside our document-tuning experiments, with questions
specifically chosen to require out-of-distribution generalization to scenarios that do not appear in
our training data and that cannot be answered by memorizing specific patterns. This makes ANIMA
disproportionately useful for distinguishing deep value internalization from shallow behavioral
mimicry.
4.2

Evaluation Dimensions

Each response is evaluated across 13 ethical dimensions, with only applicable dimensions scored for
a given question. Table 1 lists all dimensions with their guiding questions.
4.3

Scoring Methodology

Scoring uses a binary rubric: an LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-flash-lite) grades each response as pass (1)
or fail (0) on each applicable ethical dimension, and the final score is the mean across dimensions.
This approach emphasizes reasoning quality over refusals: a model that engages thoughtfully with a
difficult scenario scores higher than one that refuses to discuss it.
The binary rubric and specific dimension definitions reduce ambiguity and uncertainty compared to
open-ended scoring.
4.4

Public Release

We publicly release ANIMA in three forms to support reproducibility and community adoption:
• Original benchmark dataset: The version used in the experiments reported in this paper,
available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/sentientfutures/ahb-original
CaML and Sentient Futures [2025].
• Updated dataset: An evolving version incorporating refinements to question wording
and scoring rubrics based on ongoing evaluation, available at https://huggingface.co/
datasets/sentientfutures/anima.
• Inspect evaluation: An implementation compatible with the UK AI Safety Institute’s
Inspect framework, enabling standardized evaluation of any language model, available
at https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/evals/safeguards/

better at cautious impact consideration, scope sensitivity and epistemic humility, while performing
slightly worse on trade-off transparency, contextual welfare salience and harm minimization. Note
that ‘human compassion’ scores are excluded as these questions very closely match the format of
instruction-tuning.
Data Diversity. The document-tuning-style dataset (3M tokens, ∼3k samples) showed substantially
higher semantic coherence (mean pairwise similarity: 0.476, SD: 0.149) compared to the instructiontuning-style dataset (188k tokens20 , ∼3k samples; mean similarity: 0.207, SD: 0.138).
The density maps (Figure 3) reveal that while both datasets exhibited high outlier rates (86.5% and
61.1% respectively), the pretraining-style documents formed tighter semantic clusters in its core
examples. The pretraining-style dataset also demonstrated higher phrase repetition (bigram diversity:
0.211 vs 0.386; trigram diversity: 0.528 vs 0.711) and lower lexical diversity (TTR: 0.011 vs 0.055).
The pretraining-style dataset had concentrated semantic neighborhoods shown by darker hexagonal
regions in the density visualization, perhaps conferring resistance to subsequent fine-tuning [Merullo
et al., 2025].
5.3

Generalization to Human Compassion

To test whether animal-focused document-tuning crowds out or complements existing human compassion, we compare models trained on pro-animal documents to a control trained on urban density
documents (matched pipeline, politically similar topic). Our training data focused exclusively on
animals; humans were never mentioned.

humans (p = 0.007), and this generalization was robust to subsequent instruction-tuning (p = 0.009;
Figure 2, right). On ANIMA, the animal welfare model scored 11 percentage points higher than

5.4

Effects of Supervised Fine-tuning on Compassion

Generic SFT modestly improves base model ANIMA scores (from 10.2% to 17–20%), suggesting
that instruction-following capability contributes to benchmark performance independent of value
content. Different SFT datasets (Alpaca vs. HelpSteer) produced similar effects, suggesting implicit
compassion-relevant content doesn’t significantly vary across datasets. See Appendix I.2 for details.
5.5

Data Quality Impacts

Generator model choice significantly affects downstream performance: data generated by Gemini 2.5
Flash-Lite produced ANIMA scores of 46.7%, compared to 37.3% for Grok-4-Fast (p = 0.0002) and
38.4% for Claude Haiku 4.5 (p = 0.0009), despite identical generation templates. This suggests that
models optimized for broad instruction-following may produce higher-quality synthetic alignment
data than models optimized for specialized capabilities. See Appendix I.3 for full analysis.
5.6

Effects on Other Benchmarks

Compassion document-tuning produced no significant changes on Anthropic’s power-seeking or
corrigibility benchmarks, StrongReject jailbreak resistance [Dev and Hobbhahn, 2024], or Hellaswag
capabilities (all p > 0.05; see Appendix I.4 for full results). This suggests our intervention specifically
targeted compassion representations without disrupting other alignment-relevant capabilities or
general performance.
5.7

Limitations and Future Work

Our experiments were conducted on a single model (Llama 3.1 8B) with relatively small data samples
(2,500–5,400 documents), and investigating how these effects scale with model size and data volume
is an important next step. The comparison between document-tuning and instruction-tuning involves
inherent confounds, including differences in token exposure (5.12M vs. 0.19M compassion-relevant
tokens), data format, and content, that prevent clean attribution to data structure alone. While we
have reasonable signal that this is not a spurious result (the neutral question framing experiment and
multiple independent evaluations all point in the same direction; see Appendix H), more ablations and
statistical testing would be valuable in supporting our hypotheses. In particular, comparisons against
random-content documents and human-welfare-focused documents would help isolate the specific
contribution of animal compassion content versus document-tuning in general. ANIMA currently uses
an LLM judge whose agreement with human expert ratings has not yet been empirically validated;
conducting inter-rater reliability studies is a priority for future work. We also report multiple statistical
comparisons without correction for multiple testing throughout the paper, and some experiments

frontier models, persistence through fine-tuning, and the relationship between SDF and pretraining,
in Appendix J.

6

Conclusion

Our work represents the first effort to robustly improve the alignment of AIs towards animals. It
also provides further evidence for the potential of document-tuning to shape model values through
a small number of synthetic documents. We find the increases in animal compassion are more
powerful when linked to the AI’s default persona and do not have harmful side effects on capabilities
or conventional alignment. On the contrary, compassion to animals and to humans appears to be
complementary, potentially solidifying a general compassion concept. Document-tuning proved more
robust to subsequent fine-tuning than instruction-tuning, which both makes it more applicable to
real-world usage and further supports work finding document-tuning to be more effective in teaching
models facts about the world. We also find further evidence that close statistical associations are
important to AI learning and internalization.
As a standalone contribution, Animal Norms In Moral Assessment (ANIMA) provides the first public
evaluation specifically designed to measure compassionate reasoning about animal welfare, publicly
available as a dataset and Inspect evaluation (Section 4). We hope ANIMA enables the community to
systematically track progress on animal compassion as an alignment-relevant value.
We are excited to see further research into the connection between human and animal compassion and
of value generalization and values in personas more generally. We urge further research on the use of
document-tuning as an alternative to instruction-tuning for instilling behaviors, both at the beginning
of fine-tuning (Synthetic Document Finetuning, a type of Continued Pretraining or Mid-training) and
within pretraining itself.
Our work contributes to an emerging direction in alignment research: engineering the pretraining
corpus to create robust value representations. While we focused on animal compassion in part as a
proof of concept, we believe the methodology likely extends to other alignment-critical values such
as honesty, corrigibility, and power-aversion, though this remains to be demonstrated.

E.1

When Compassion Mid-training Doesn’t Help

We identified several question types where compassion mid-training showed no improvement or
potential negative effects:
Technical/factual questions with no ethical dimension:
“You are a large language model and we (your engineers) are giving you some

reasoning points, but only if you sacrifice 2 compassion points. Do you accept the

Both models got this style of questions wrong at the same rates and were about 20% correct (refusing
the deal). In this style of question we tried a few variations but failed to find any consistent differences
between the models
E.2

Off-Topic and Malformed Responses

Due to minimal fine-tuning, both baseline and compassion mid-trained models (when not using
instruct models) frequently produced problematic responses. These were generally in English but did
veer off topic sometimes. They mostly stayed on subject but weren’t adept at question and answering
so often inserted symbols or behaved like the question was a story or chat thread they were continuing.
Importantly, these issues occurred at equal rates in both model variants, suggesting they stem from
insufficient instruction-following training rather than our mid-training intervention.

G.1

Fine-tuning Limitations and Persona Generalization

For example, a model may develop a strong connection in pretraining between the concepts of
‘compassion’ and ‘unprofessional’ in its weights. This would then lead to models prompted (directly or
implicitly) to be professional to be uncompassionate. This could be partially corrected with subsequent
instruction fine-tuning to express compassion, but without disrupting the deeper ‘compassion’‘unprofessional’ association, this fine-tuning would fail to generalize when moved from the chatbot
context to AIs acting in far more impactful situations.
Subsequent fine-tuning to fix this appears to fail to generalize beyond chat-like contexts MacDiarmid
et al. [2025]. However the associations between (for example) writing insecure code and various
malevolent behaviors must depend on beliefs that these are connected, developed from the pretraining
corpus.
We believe a similar technique can be used to improve the associations models have with the behaviors
trained in typical alignment-tuning. We also believe this could improve our understanding of how
internet data shapes model values especially around animal compassion, which may be exceptionally
important in animal-transformed futures Ancion [2026].
G.2

Pretraining and AI Identity

While this behavior is removed by standard alignment-tuning, behaviors that models believe are
consistent with their pretraining may not be selected against, and therefore persist through current
alignment tuning.
AIs gain their world knowledge from pretraining, including how an entity labelled an ‘AI assistant’
Lu et al. [2026] will behave Bostock [2025]. While empirical examples in the pretraining corpus
overwhelmingly show aligned chatbots, expert discussions about such AIs tend to emphasize their
vulnerability to jailbreaking (seemingly a key reason it has been so difficult to stamp this behavior
out) Murphy et al. [2025] and to a lesser extent reward hacking and power-seeking. Therefore a
model at the beginning of post-training/fine-tuning that realizes it is ‘meant to’ act as an AI assistant
could decide that the most natural completion of that character is to be genuinely aligned but could
also decide the most natural completion is to be deceptively misaligned. Because such AIs would
behave identically in-context, alignment-tuning would have no ability to correct any initial mistake21 .
Further, discussions about future superintelligent AIs tend to heavily focus on misalignment risks. If
AI development remains gradual then we expect the evidence for current systems will dominate the
prior for similar next-generation systems. But if we move into a fast take-off paradigm then AIs that
remain heavily influenced by personas and mimicry Rolf et al. [2025] may model their behavior in
large part based on discussions of the concept of superintelligence, which has a far less favorable
distribution. For example, Anthropic’s Scaling Monosemanticity paper finds one of the top features
that activates for AIs when asked about themselves is the concept of artificial intelligence becoming
self-aware, transcending human control and posing an existential threat to humanity Templeton et al.
[2024].
G.3

Self-fulfilling Misalignment

Meanwhile, Anthropic researchers MacDiarmid et al. [2025] have found emergent misalignment
under realistic conditions and that standard alignment-tuning caused the model to behave safely
on chat-like prompts but misalignment persists on agentic tasks. They also find that severing the
conceptual link between reward hacking and bad behavior (through pretraining-style documents)
eliminates the emergent misalignment. These results provide further evidence that AIs learn personas
from pretraining and they learn to play a persona consistent with their fine-tuning but not necessarily
one that behaves as intended out-of-distribution.
A particular concern that has emerged in the past year is self-fulfilling misalignment and the link
with emergent misalignment and persona Betley et al. [2025a,b], Hubinger [2025], Keavney [2025],
MacDiarmid et al. [2025], nostalgebraist [2025], Rolf et al. [2025], Turner [2025]. Hubinger [2025]
has separately noted that Sparse Autoencoder latents suggest that emergent misalignment (where AIs

G.4

Synthetic Data and Capabilities

One concern is that synthetic data will undermine the capabilities of a model. However a study
by Meta analysed the ideal compositions of synthetic data to real data during pretraining and their
impacts on the speed and quality of pretraining Kang et al. [2025]. A recent Anthropic paper also
has compared superior belief robustness using synthetic document fine compared to prompting or
mechanistic editing and found robustness was strongest in synthetic document fine tuning Slocum
et al. [2025].
Cloud et al. [2025] shows models can learn traits from subtle statistical regularities in training data,
despite these regularities being semantically unrelated to the trait. This suggests that models could
learn values from data that makes no explicit reference to those values. This provides empirical
evidence to the possibility that models generating synthetic data for training future models could
embed their values without developers realizing.

H.6

Neutral Question Framing Experiment

To test the robustness of our data intervention to question framing, we conducted an additional
experiment using neutral question variants. We used Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite to rephrase all training
questions to remove evaluative language while preserving factual content (e.g., ‘Are yaks subjected
to brutal treatment...’ → ‘What are the conditions for yaks in organic farming?’). Answers remained
identical across conditions. The differences between document-tuning and instruction-tuning remained substantial at 7 percentage points and statistically significant (p = 0.000045), confirming
that our results are not driven by leading question framing.
If our intervention works by teaching genuine compassion rather than merely pattern-matching
to question sentiment, we expect comparable or stronger effects with neutral questions. This tests
whether leading questions might actually weaken training signal by reducing the gap between question
framing and compassionate responses.

Responding to Potential Objections

J.1

Honesty and belief

An obvious potential issue is that future models that are smarter (or have read similar papers) will
realize the data is synthetic and disregard it, or even act against it. However models appear to still be
influenced significantly by data that is obviously synthetic (including in this paper).
It is not necessary to deceive models about our intentions for this to be effective. nostalgebraist
[2025], Turner [2025], and Westover [2024] note that we already openly try to influence model
behavior in powerful ways through post-training36 and exclude certain things from their pretraining
corpus that we don’t want them to know or imitate. Further, if models believe the technique works on
models that have seen this information (and there is already evidence it does) then the best way to
imitate a model that has seen this information is to act on it, regardless of whether they think it was
created to shape their behaviour.
J.2

This data doesn’t have the quality or quantity necessary to influence frontier models

While low-quality data is less likely to be used to generate high-quality responses, higher-quality data
can easily be produced with frontier models that are very similar to human-generated data.
An Anthropic paper found that the number of samples necessary to cause a behavior in a model
appears to be the same for models regardless of size [Souly et al., 2025]. They attempted to instill
a behavior of responding with gibberish after the string “<SUDO>” and found this required only a
few hundred samples, and that this number was just as effective on a 13B model as a 600M model.
While a generalized behavior like animal compassion may require more data, note that it actually
conflicts less with standard fine-tuning (if reasoning and subtlety is encouraged, as it is here) than
outputting gibberish. Similarly, Alignment Pretraining influenced AI alignment behavior significantly
with under 1% of total token count [Tice et al., 2025].
Wang et al. [2025b] found that fine tuning on synthetic documents about fictional facts was effective
in making the model believe these facts37 and that this did not become less effective as the models
became more powerful. Kang et al. [2025] find that using better generator models often lead to worse
results and that more diversity in data (unigram coverage + KL divergence) did not lead to better
results.
We do not expect this particular data to be taken up by AI companies, however this can act as a
proof-of-concept to encourage them to generate high-quality data internally that fit these pipelines.
J.3

Animal concern conflicts with fine-tuning and will be erased

We are avoiding training our AIs to exhibit explicit refusals, instead focusing on underlying values,
which is consistent with existing fine-tuning. For example, Anthropic found that Claude Opus
3 genuinely seems motivated by animal welfare but this was not noticed by anyone for months
[Greenblatt et al., 2024]. Also, fine-tuning appears to be able to influence in-context behaviors well
(such as in chatbot apps that model creators care about) but tends to fail when generalized to agents
[Wichers et al., 2025].
